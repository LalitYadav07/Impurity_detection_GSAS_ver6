RADAR-PD user guideExperiment Planner · 0.7.0

Before you measure

Plan your experiment

Open Experiment Planner beside Analysis. You can work without an IPTS or measured diffraction file. Returning to Analysis preserves its selected experiment and view.

1. Choose the instrument and calibration

Select the beamline at the top of the page. POWGEN is the only supported option for now. Then choose a calibration cycle and an instrument configuration. The application supplies its packaged GSAS-II instrument parameters. The supported 2026B high-resolution 60 Hz wavelength bands also supply measured response, background and numerical resolution tables. They are selected together; the planner does not guess a response for an unmatched configuration.

Prediction distinguishes count predictions from a pattern-shape preview. Historical configurations and custom .instprm or .prm files offer a shape preview. A custom file defines peak position and profile parameters; it does not, by itself, calibrate incident flux or counts. No live archive access is required, and newly published cycle files are not fetched automatically.

2. Describe the sample

Upload the nuclear CIF or use Try silicon example. Enter powder mass, cylinder radius, packing fraction and container. Under Illumination, enter the beam width and height. The powder fill height follows from mass, crystal density, radius and packing. These values are assumptions unless you have measured them.

Under Isotope composition, read the structure to reveal the GSAS-II isotope choices. Natural abundance is the default. For mixtures, specify atom percentages adding to 100%. Additional crystalline phases use mass percentages, with the remaining mass assigned to the primary CIF. Transitions affect the primary phase.

3. Optionally explore a transition

Magnetic subgroup: enter one commensurate propagation vector. Find magnetic subgroups, select an embedding and magnetic sites, then choose the magnetic ion/form factor. Prepare the moments to see the actual GSAS-II magnetic sites and independent variables. Enter their values in μB; read-only site components and moment magnitudes update from the native symmetry constraints. The project’s magnetic settings are preserved; otherwise GSAS-II defaults apply (its native Landé g default is 2, not an oxidation-state lookup). The existing Bilbao discovery and native GSAS-II symmetry preparation are reused. The calculation does not refine these values against data. Bilbao receives the parent symmetry operations and propagation vector; its service key is supplied by the application administrator.

Structural subgroup: enter the propagation vector, find structural subgroups, choose a child and prepare its displacement modes. ISODISTORT receives the parent CIF. The initial search constructs one highest-symmetry direction per irreducible representation; its catalog retains deferred or failed branches. This is not an exhaustive construction of every distortion. Each signed amplitude lists the atoms and coordinate directions it moves. Expand Resulting atomic positions to inspect the child coordinates. The inherited child lattice is shown and can be copied into the optional edit fields; edited values must preserve the selected symmetry. Zero-mode controls check that the transformed child reproduces its parent.

After simulating a structural state, Add magnetic ordering to this result uses the child as the magnetic parent and preserves the original nuclear-cell normalization. Alternatively upload an MCIF with moments, or a second nuclear CIF for a structure comparison. This compares calculated structures, not experimental measurements.

4. Choose exposure and simulate

Use cumulative proton charge, counting time and average delivered current, or time and beam power. Enter increasing comma-separated exposures, for example 60, 300, 1800 seconds. Average current should include beam interruptions. The current-cycle response uses 1.3 GeV protons. Range, binning and random seed are under the advanced controls.

Click Simulate pattern. The plot shows the expectation and a seeded synthetic observation. Choose an exposure and switch between normalized intensity and modeled raw counts. The default ±1σ bars include counting noise and finite calibration/reference uncertainty, matching exported errors. Select Sample counting only to isolate counting noise, or Error-bar size (σ) to inspect both uncertainties directly. The shaded band marks the expected pattern ± the selected per-bin standard deviation; it is not a confidence band on all physical-model assumptions. Cumulative exposures share the same simulated acquisition, so their noise is correlated as it would be for nested observations. Click legend entries to show components, drag to zoom, and double-click to reset.

Read the prediction honestly

Before the first simulation, the prediction panel explains the structure calculation, sample-dependent scale, measured instrument response, exposure and noise model. This explanation remains available beneath completed plots.

The 2026B count response is a conditional prediction, with experimental validation pending. It includes the configured sample, absorption, illumination, measured instrument response/background and count statistics. A successful calculation or software test does not establish an independently verified absolute intensity. The reference powder masses and fill heights have not been independently verified. Broader experimental agreement, particularly transition intensities and systematic uncertainty, remains a separate validation task.

Read Assumptions and calculation details for the actual calculation and its restrictions. Peak widths and counting noise do not include every possible defect, sample environment, disorder or instrument change. A shape preview provides no count-time promise. Qualifications remain attached to exported plots and results.

Carry the setup into your experiment

Under Use this plan, choose Use inputs for preparation to prepare reusable calculations, or Use inputs for analysis setup to populate the Live setup. Review the parent CIF, instrument profile, physical source bank and fitting range before applying the draft. The planner's simulated curve is never submitted as a measured scan, and applying a draft starts no jobs.

For a transition plan, explicitly choose whether to carry the original nuclear parent. Isotope mixtures, additional phases and other settings that the destination cannot preserve are listed before applying; export the full plan to keep those assumptions. Resolve any blocked settings before continuing. In Live, select the actual IPTS and sample, check the inputs, then start monitoring.

Save, reopen and export

Name the plan and choose Save plan. Select a saved plan and Open to restore inputs, transition state and its verified result. Editing an input invalidates the old plotted result until you simulate again. Saved plans are stored in the current Galaxy History. Use Refresh saved plans in a fresh session, select the saved plan and choose Open. The local standalone workspace can also retain local snapshots.

Download plan exports the input structures, settings and available results. Open uploaded plan restores this portable copy; imported native projects are treated as data, not executable authority. Transition preparation may need to be repeated. Download patterns and provenance also preserves the completed simulation and calculation receipt.

Simulations and native transition preparation run as Galaxy scientific jobs. You can change workspace tabs while they run. Under Saved work, downloads and reconnecting, choose Find previous calculations, select a saved operation and Reconnect to calculation. Reconnection retrieves the existing job; it does not launch a duplicate. A changed input or engine invalidates a previous prediction until it has been recalculated. Cancel preserves your inputs; an external discovery request already in progress can take up to 60 seconds to return.
